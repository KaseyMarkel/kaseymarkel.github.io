How dense is San Francisco, really?

San Francisco feels packed, and there’s a claim going around that part of it is among the densest places in America. It holds up. I counted all 8.2 million 2020 Census blocks and slid squares of 1, 2, 3, 5 and 10 km across the whole country. By homes, the area around the Tenderloin and Nob Hill is the densest in the US outside New York City at every size from 1 to 5 km (at 10 km, Chicago edges ahead). Its densest square kilometre holds about 25,800 homes. Only ten squares in the US have more, all of them in Manhattan.

But it’s dense in homes more than in people, and the density doesn’t spread far. Those homes are small: about 1.5 residents each, against about 2 in Barcelona’s densest square. SF’s densest square kilometre has about 39,700 residents. In the four countries I checked, 78 squares beat it: 27 in New York, 44 in Barcelona, its neighbouring towns, Madrid and Valencia, and 7 in Paris. Zoom out to a 10 km square, about the size of the city, and SF’s best holds about 8,200 residents per km² (it’s 98% land, so water isn’t the excuse). London’s best holds 12,300, Madrid’s and Barcelona’s about 18,900, New York’s 19,300 and Paris’s 21,700. Cities built out with mid-rise apartments fit two to three times as many people on the same ground.

How it was made

For each country I took the finest official counts available. For the US, all 2020 Census blocks. For England and Wales, 2021 Census output areas. For Spain, 2021 Census sections. For France, INSEE’s 2021 Filosofi 200 m grid. I placed each small area at one point and slid squares across the map in 100 m steps on an equal-area projection. The ranking keeps the densest square, drops everything overlapping it, and repeats, so the top 50 aren’t 50 copies of one Manhattan block.

A few things aren’t like-for-like. “Homes” means every housing unit in the US and every dwelling in Spain, vacant ones included, but only occupied homes in England, Wales and France. So people per km² is the fairer comparison across countries. The French grid leaves out people living in institutions and student halls (it covers 94% of Paris’s census population), so Paris is slightly undercounted. Densities are per km² of the whole square, water included. The Land % column shows how much of each square is land; it’s approximate, because each area’s land counts at its centre point. Scotland and Northern Ireland aren’t included.
